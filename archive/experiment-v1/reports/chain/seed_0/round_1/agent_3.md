## The Free-Fermion Mirage

**Critical flaw**: Zinn-Justin's fermionic realization of KTW puzzles operates at the *generating function* level, not at the level of individual bijections. The free-fermion vertex model produces the correct *counts* but doesn't automatically yield bijective correspondences between puzzle configurations and tableaux.

The "fermionic superposition" interpretation of nabla vertices fundamentally misunderstands the combinatorial problem. In Purbhoo's migration, each nabla choice corresponds to a *deterministic* local move (a specific jeu de taquin slide direction). The "4:1 multiplicity" isn't quantum superposition—it's the classical fact that multiple puzzle configurations can yield identical tableaux under migration.

**Concrete challenge**: Show me how fermionic creation/annihilation operators resolve the specific issue that a single KTW rhombus can participate in multiple overlapping constraints simultaneously. The vertex model formalism doesn't address this geometric rigidity.

## Transfer Matrix Misidentification

**Deeper error**: The analysis conflates "having a transfer matrix representation" with "admitting natural growth diagram dynamics." These are distinct properties.

Yes, KTW puzzles likely emerge from some vertex model with commuting transfer matrices [T(u),T(v)]=0. But growth diagrams require something stronger: the transfer matrix evolution must correspond to *local face rules* that build tableaux incrementally. The commutativity ensures consistent counts, not constructive bijections.

**The boundary condition trap**: "Boundary R-matrices" don't resolve the triangular geometry issue—they typically assume the boundary can be embedded in a periodic or rectangular setting. KTW puzzles have genuinely triangular constraints that resist such embedding without artificial padding that destroys the natural correspondence.

## Conservation Laws vs. Constructive Combinatorics

The fermionic number conservation analogy reveals a fundamental confusion between *global invariants* and *local constructibility*.

In RSK-type growth diagrams, we can always determine the next face label from local information alone. But rhombus conservation in KTW puzzles creates *non-local* dependencies: whether a given rhombus configuration is allowed depends on the global puzzle topology, not just local vertex conditions.

**Challenge the counter-conjecture**: If fermion number conservation naturally gives local growth rules, explain why the migration algorithm requires the complex hexagonal flip sequences rather than simple local updates. The migration's indirectness suggests the conservation structure doesn't decompose locality-compatibly.

## The Six-Vertex Phase Diagram Red Herring

**Missing the point**: Even if KTW puzzles correspond to the free-fermion point (Δ=0), this doesn't guarantee growth diagram compatibility. The six-vertex model at Δ=0 gives *perfect matching* enumerations, but perfect matchings and growth diagrams solve different combinatorial problems.

Perfect matchings count weighted configurations. Growth diagrams provide explicit bijections with transparent intermediate objects. The analysis assumes these naturally coincide, but they often don't—as evidenced by the complexity of migration.

## The Schutzenberger Connection Blindness

**Most serious omission**: The analysis completely ignores how growth diagrams must interface with Schutzenberger involution to be meaningful for LR-tableaux.

In the RSK growth diagram, Schutzenberger involution corresponds to reflection across the main diagonal. The vertex model framework provides no guidance for this geometric constraint.
