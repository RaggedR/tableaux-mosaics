**FUNDAMENTAL CONTRADICTION**: This vertex model critique contains a devastating internal inconsistency. ### Yang-Baxter Equation: Category Error

**Wrong algebraic structure entirely**: The critique conflates two incompatible frameworks. Yang-Baxter equations govern **braid group representations** and **quantum group actions**. But Littlewood-Richardson coefficients arise from **Schur function multiplication** in the **ring of symmetric functions**—a completely different algebraic context where YBE is irrelevant.

**Spectral parameter confusion compounds**: The author's "rapidity differences" language borrows from **statistical mechanics** models (6-vertex model, XXZ chain) but KTW puzzles encode **Grassmannian cohomology**. These aren't "interaction energies" but **intersection multiplicities**. The boundary conditions λ,μ,ν aren't "spectral parameters"—they're **Schubert class labels**.

### Jeu de Taquin vs. Vertex Models: Fundamentally Different Operations

**The real obstruction missed**: The critique ignores that Purbhoo's migration algorithm uses **jeu de taquin slides**, which have *no* vertex model interpretation. Jdt operates on **tableau entries** (integers 1,2,3,...), while vertex models operate on **spin configurations** (±1, or more generally, representations of quantum groups).

**Local vs. global dynamics confusion**: The "six-vertex interaction neighborhoods" claim misunderstands puzzle geometry. Rhombi interact through **constraint propagation** across the entire puzzle, not through local vertex weights. This is why migration requires **global hexagon flips**, not local vertex updates.

### Growth Diagrams: RSK vs. Tropical Confusion

**RSK operates on matrices, not lattice paths**: The critique's "insertion vs recording" distinction reveals fundamental misunderstanding. RSK correspondence relates **permutation matrices** to **tableau pairs**. **Infinite-dimensional claim is nonsense**: Puzzle configurations for fixed boundary data λ,μ,ν form a **finite set** (this is how LR-coefficients are finite integers!). The "infinite-dimensional configuration spaces" claim contradicts basic enumerative combinatorics.

**Growth diagrams require unique face labels**: In classical growth diagrams (RSK, Edelman-Greene, etc.), each lattice square gets a **unique integer label** determined by local rules. But puzzle-to-tableau correspondence is **many-to-many**: multiple puzzles can yield the same LR-tableau, and vice versa.
