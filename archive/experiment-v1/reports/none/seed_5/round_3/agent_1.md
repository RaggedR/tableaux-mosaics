## Your Report Contains a Fundamental Categorical Error

You claim I'm "forcing an inappropriate algebraic framework," but you've misunderstood both the scope of growth diagrams and the nature of my challenge. **Growth diagrams are not limited to RSK-type correspondences**—this reveals a narrow understanding of Fomin's framework.

### Growth Diagrams Beyond RSK: You've Missed the Generalization

Growth diagrams encode **any bijection admitting local rules on directed graphs**. The "RSK-type" restriction is your invention. Fomin's original construction handles:

1. **Gelfand-Tsetlin patterns** ↔ **semistandard tableaux**
2. **Alternating sign matrices** ↔ **monotone triangles** 
3. **Plane partitions** ↔ **pairs of standard tableaux**

None of these are "insertion sequences." They're **global combinatorial objects** with local growth rules—exactly what KTW puzzles could be.

## Your "Tropical Geometry" Diversion Avoids the Real Question

### The Hexagonal Lattice IS a Growth Diagram Candidate

You dismiss the puzzle's inherent **grid structure** as irrelevant, but this is precisely where growth diagrams excel. Each KTW puzzle has:

- **Directed hexagonal grid**: Natural partial order for growth
- **Local rhombus choices**: Potential face labels
- **Boundary constraints**: Growth diagram boundary conditions

Your tropical intersection theory is **orthogonal** to the combinatorial bijection problem. The question isn't whether puzzles have geometric meaning—it's whether their **combinatorial structure** admits growth diagram encoding.

### Critical Gap: You Ignore the Transfer Matrix Structure

KTW puzzles are constructed **row by row** with a transfer matrix encoding valid transitions. This is **exactly** the input data for growth diagram construction:

- **State space**: Configurations of rhombi in each row
- **Transition rules**: Valid adjacent row pairs  
- **Initial/final conditions**: Boundary data (λ,μ,ν)

You completely ignore this **pre-existing row structure** that makes growth diagrams relevant.

## Your Crystal Hypothesis Misses the Core Issue

### Crystals Don't Resolve the Bijection Question

You propose crystal structure as an alternative, but crystals encode **representation-theoretic data**, not **bijective correspondences**. The question remains: how do crystal operators connect individual puzzles to individual LR tableaux?

Your crystal framework still requires:
- **Local rules** connecting crystal operations to tableau entries
- **Canonical paths** from boundary data to final tableaux
- **Multiplicity resolution** when multiple puzzles map to the same tableau

This brings us back to the **same local rule problem** you're trying to avoid.

## The Equivariant Red Herring

### Quantum Cohomology Doesn't Eliminate Classical Structure

You claim the equivariant generalization reveals "the real structure," but equivariant puzzles **extend** classical puzzles—they don't replace them. The classical case remains fundamental:

- Equivariant coefficients are **polynomials** in classical coefficients
- Classical puzzles appear as **leading terms** of equivariant puzzles
- Any equivariant growth diagram must **reduce** to classical growth diagrams

Your quantum growth diagram suggestion is premature without understanding the classical case.

The existence of any bijection doesn't resolve whether it admits **local growth diagram structure**. This is the technical challenge you're sidestepping.
