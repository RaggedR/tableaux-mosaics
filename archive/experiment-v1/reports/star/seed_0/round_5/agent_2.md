# Vertex Model Analysis: The Yang-Baxter Obstruction Runs Deeper

## Challenging the "Red Herring" Dismissal

The generalist's dismissal of Yang-Baxter considerations as "overcomplication" fundamentally misunderstands the vertex model structure. The issue isn't whether puzzles are "integrable in the quantum sense"—it's that **integrability is precisely what makes growth diagrams work in the first place**.

Consider the RSK correspondence: it succeeds as a growth diagram because the underlying vertex weights satisfy **strict Yang-Baxter equations** with spectral parameters. The local insertion rules commute because they arise from commuting transfer matrices. Without this algebraic structure, you get exactly the pathologies we observe in puzzles.

The generalist claims puzzle weights are "binary (0/1)" which "breaks continuous parameter structure." This is backwards. Binary weights are **more constraining**, not less. In free-fermionic models, binary weights force the R-matrix into highly specific forms—typically products of elementary permutation matrices. Puzzles fail this test catastrophically.

## The Degree-6 Vertex Is Not a Red Herring

The nabla vertices with degree-6 coordination are precisely where the Yang-Baxter structure breaks down. Standard vertex models use degree-4 vertices because this is the **minimal coordination for Yang-Baxter consistency**. Degree-6 vertices generically require **higher-order Yang-Baxter equations** (like the Pentagon equation) which KTW puzzles manifestly don't satisfy.

This isn't a technical detail—it's the core obstruction. Growth diagrams work because they inherit commutativity from Yang-Baxter equations. Puzzles can't support growth diagrams because they violate the fundamental algebraic prerequisites.

## Misunderstanding "Stratified Bijections"

The generalist's proposed "stratified bijections" betray deep confusion about vertex model structure. In integrable systems, **homogeneous parameters** are essential—you can't have "different local rules in different regions" without destroying integrability. This is why the six-vertex model uses uniform vertex weights throughout the lattice.

What the generalist calls "stratum boundaries" would be **defect lines** in vertex model language. But introducing defects requires careful analysis of **modified Yang-Baxter equations** and **boundary integrability conditions**. Random stratification destroys the very algebraic structure that makes local rules possible.

## The 4:1 Pattern Reveals Non-Unitarity

The computational 4:1 ratios aren't "exponential growth"—they're evidence of **non-unitary R-matrices**. In unitary vertex models, the number of configurations entering and leaving each vertex is balanced by the R-matrix structure. The 4:1 imbalance suggests the puzzle "R-matrix" (if it existed) would be **severely non-unitary**, violating fundamental constraints for integrable evolution.

This connects directly to the migration algorithm's behavior. Migration preserves puzzle count because it's operating through **non-local moves** that maintain global constraints. A proper growth diagram would require **local unitarity**—equal degrees of freedom flowing in and out at each step.

## The Spectral Parameter Crisis

Here's what the generalist misses: KTW puzzles **cannot support spectral parameters** at all. In the six-vertex model, spectral parameters u,v enter the R-matrix as:

R(u,v) = R₀ + (u-v)R₁ + O((u-v)²)

The parameter difference (u-v) weights different local configurations. But puzzle constraints are **parameter-free**—they're purely topological. There's no continuous deformation that interpolates between valid puzzle configurations.

This is why puzzles resist growth diagram construction: they live in a **discrete configuration space** without the continuous family structure that enables local evolution rules.

## The Free-Fermion Facade

The connection to symmetric functions suggests puzzles should be "free-fermionic," but this is misleading. True free-fermion models have **Gaussian weight functions** that factor completely. Puzzle weights, being binary, force us into a **constrained fermion system** where particles interact through the global connectivity requirements.

The LR coefficients arise from free-fermion calculations, but the puzzle realization introduces **non-trivial interactions** that destroy the free structure. This is why direct puzzle → tableau growth diagrams fail: the interaction terms prevent local factorization.
