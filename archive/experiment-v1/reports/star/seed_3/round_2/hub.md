# Synthesis: The Growth Diagram Mirage

## The Core Tension: Local Rules vs. Global Constraints

All four perspectives converge on a fundamental problem: **KTW puzzles encode global geometric constraints that resist local decomposition**. The geometric specialist correctly identifies that puzzles are "global tilings with boundary constraints," while the vertex model theorist acknowledges this as "boundary-to-boundary compatibility." The tableau specialist emphasizes that migration involves "non-local jdt dynamics," and the computational evidence shows that "any local rule must access non-local boundary information."

This creates an immediate tension with growth diagram methodology, which **requires** local face-labeling rules. The question isn't just whether such a bijection exists—it's whether the very framework makes sense for this problem.

## Three Distinct Mathematical Objects Masquerading as One

A critical gap emerges when we examine what each specialist means by "transfer matrix":

- **Geometric**: Boolean constraint propagators on configuration spaces
- **Vertex Model**: Spectral operators T(u) satisfying RTT relations  
- **Tableau**: Recording devices for jeu de taquin slide sequences
- **Computational**: Sparse matrices over F₂ with no meaningful spectrum

These aren't different views of the same object—they're **completely different mathematical structures** that happen to share terminology. The growth diagram question conflates them, creating conceptual confusion.

## The 12→3 "Compression" Reveals Structural Incompatibility

Each specialist interprets this differently, but their explanations are mutually contradictory:

- **Geometric**: "Highly constrained local rules with mutual constraints"
- **Vertex Model**: "Yang-Baxter equation forcing compatibility" 
- **Tableau**: "Not compression but algorithmic unfolding of jdt slides"
- **Computational**: "Constraint satisfaction rejecting inconsistent configurations"

The fact that four specialists give four incompatible explanations for the same phenomenon suggests the growth diagram framework is **forcing inappropriate conceptualizations**. The 12→3 relationship isn't a single mathematical fact to be explained—it's an artifact of trying to unify incompatible structures.

## The False Promise of "Directness"

All specialists implicitly challenge the premise that direct = better:

- **Geometric**: "Direct bijection would require geometric rectification between coordinate systems"
- **Vertex Model**: "The migration algorithm IS the Yang-Baxter equation in disguise"
- **Tableau**: "Puzzle→mosaic→migration→tableau chain is algorithmically optimal"
- **Computational**: "Direct enumeration might outperform migration"

This reveals a fundamental misunderstanding in the problem statement. The question assumes that eliminating intermediate steps necessarily improves the mathematics. But the specialists suggest the opposite: **the intermediate steps serve essential structural purposes**.

## Missing Framework: Categorical Coherence

None of the specialists addresses a crucial question: **Do puzzles and LR tableaux even live in the same category?** 

- Puzzles are geometric objects (tilings with boundary conditions)
- LR tableaux are combinatorial objects (words with algebraic constraints)
- Growth diagrams are dynamic processes (local rules generating global structures)

The bijection question assumes these categories can be naturally related, but the specialists' struggles suggest they might be **categorically incompatible**. A growth diagram would need to bridge not just different representations, but different mathematical universes.

## The Computational Falsifiability Gap

The computational specialist offers concrete predictions, but the other three provide no falsifiable claims. This creates an asymmetric dialogue where computational evidence can potentially refute geometric/algebraic intuitions, but not vice versa.

**Critical gap**: None of the specialists address what would constitute **proof** that no growth diagram exists. The geometric specialist suggests "ill-posed," the vertex model theorist pivots to spectral curves, the tableau specialist dismisses the question, and only the computational specialist offers testable predictions.

## Alternative Synthesis: The Puzzle as Boundary Condition

Here's a unifying perspective the specialists missed: **What if puzzles aren't input data but boundary conditions for a growth process?**

Instead of asking how to build tableaux FROM puzzles, ask: what growth process has puzzles as its BOUNDARY CONDITIONS? This reframes the entire question:

- The "growth" isn't puzzle→tableau but boundary→interior
- The "diagram" records interior states consistent with puzzle boundaries  
- The "transfer matrix" propagates boundary constraints inward
- The LR tableau emerges as the unique interior state
