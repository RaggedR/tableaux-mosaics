# Growth Diagram Specialist Analysis: The Synthesis Misses the Local Rule Crisis

## The False Equivalence Problem

The synthesis commits a fundamental error by treating "face label dimension" as merely an information-theoretic constraint. This misunderstands the **mathematical necessity** of local rules in growth diagrams. Face labels aren't arbitrary data containers—they encode **vertex weight functions** that satisfy **strict compatibility conditions**.

The suggestion that face labels could encode "jeu de taquin slide instructions" reveals deep confusion. **Local rules must be invertible functions of immediately adjacent data**. Jeu de taquin slides are **non-local operations** that can affect arbitrarily distant tableau entries. You cannot encode a non-local operation as a local rule without violating the growth diagram axioms.

## The Cylindric Construction: A Red Herring

The synthesis elevates my offhand comment about cylindric variants to a "crucial unexplored direction," but this demonstrates misunderstanding of the **boundary condition problem**. Cylindric growth diagrams don't eliminate boundary conditions—they **change their topology**. Changing the surface topology doesn't resolve this logical incompatibility. Whether on a disk, cylinder, or torus, you still need **deterministic local rules**, and puzzle configurations still have **genuine choice points**.

## The Real Obstruction: Non-Locality of Puzzle Constraints

The synthesis repeatedly conflates **computational complexity** with **structural incompatibility**. The fundamental problem isn't that puzzles are "exponentially complex"—it's that **puzzle constraints are inherently non-local**.

A puzzle configuration's validity depends on **global constraint satisfaction** across the entire triangular region. Growth diagrams require **local constraint propagation** where each face depends only on immediately adjacent data. These are **logically incompatible requirements**.
