# Synthesis Report: The Growth Diagram Conjecture Under Critical Analysis

## Core Tension: Algorithmic vs. Structural Perspectives

The four specialist reports reveal a fundamental divide between **algorithmic computation** (what can be calculated) and **structural necessity** (what must be true mathematically). The growth diagram conjecture fails not due to a single obstruction, but due to **misaligned mathematical architectures**.

The computational specialist correctly identifies three orthogonal failure modes, while the others seek deeper structural explanations. This tension exposes a critical gap: **we're asking whether fundamentally different mathematical objects can be unified, not just whether they're bijectively equivalent**.

However, the vertex model specialist's challenge is valid - this assumes face labels must encode **final tableaux content** rather than **intermediate evolution rules**. The computational experiments should test whether face labels can encode:
- Jeu de taquin slide instructions (migration specialist's suggestion)
- Statistical correlation data (computational alternative)

## Critical Oversight: The Categorical Universe Problem

All specialists miss a fundamental issue: **puzzles and tableaux don't just have different combinatorial structures - they live in different categorical universes**. 

- **Puzzles**: Static constraint satisfaction problems on fixed geometric domains
- **Tableaux**: Dynamic insertion processes with evolving boundary conditions
- **Growth diagrams**: Local evolution operators preserving global invariants

The migration process isn't "indirect" - it's a **category-theoretic functor** between fundamentally incompatible mathematical contexts. The synthesis question assumes these categories can be unified, but this may be mathematically impossible, not just computationally difficult.

## The Yang-Baxter Red Herring vs. Real Constraint

The vertex model specialist's Yang-Baxter obstruction, while technically sophisticated, addresses the wrong level of structure. The computational specialist correctly identifies this as "scaffolding, not essence." 

The **real constraint** is simpler and more fundamental: growth diagrams require **deterministic local rules**, but puzzle configurations have **genuine non-deterministic choice points** (nabla assignments). This isn't about integrability - it's about **logical decidability**.

**Alternative framing**: Instead of asking "can we make a growth diagram," ask "what additional structure eliminates non-determinism in puzzle configurations?"

## Missing Experimental Framework: Topological Variants

The growth diagram specialist's suggestion about **cylindric puzzle variants** reveals a crucial unexplored direction. The boundary condition incompatibility (vertex model obstruction) might be purely topological rather than algebraic.

**Testable hypothesis**: Study puzzle analogues on:
- Cylindric surfaces (periodic horizontally)
- Toroidal surfaces (doubly periodic)  
- Projective planes (different boundary behavior)

If growth diagrams emerge naturally in these contexts, the obstruction is **topological constraint**, not fundamental incompatibility.

## The Migration Reality: Algorithmic Necessity vs. Geometric Inevitability

The migration specialist's "concrete jeu de taquin slides" vs. the generalist's "geometric necessity" represent different levels of mathematical description. Both are correct, but address different questions:

- **Algorithmic level**: How do we compute the bijection?
- **Structural level**: Why must this computation take this specific form?

The growth diagram conjecture fails at the **structural level** because it assumes algorithmic computability implies structural unification. Migration reveals these are **distinct mathematical requirements**.
