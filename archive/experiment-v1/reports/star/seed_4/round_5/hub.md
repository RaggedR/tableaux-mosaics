# Mathematical Synthesis: The Growth Diagram Conjecture's Critical Failures

## Core Tension: Locality vs. Global Coherence

The specialist reports converge on a fundamental incompatibility: **KTW puzzles require global constraint satisfaction while growth diagrams depend on local rule systems**. This isn't merely a technical obstacle—it reveals a category error in the original conjecture.

The algebraic specialist correctly identifies that puzzle validity requires checking edge-coloring consistency across the entire triangular domain, violating the locality principle that makes growth diagrams tractable. The vertex model specialist reinforces this by showing that free-fermionic systems cannot "detect" the non-local constraints that define valid puzzles. The computational analysis provides the quantitative dimension: information compression ratios grow exponentially, making constant-size face labels mathematically impossible.

**Key insight**: The growth diagram framework assumes **spatially local** interactions, but KTW puzzles embed **topologically global** constraints. This mismatch runs deeper than boundary conditions or Yang-Baxter compatibility.

## The Yang-Baxter Equation: Wrong Type, Not Wrong Concept

The specialists agree Yang-Baxter is non-negotiable but identify a crucial gap: **everyone has been studying the wrong Yang-Baxter equation**. The tableaux specialist's observation that puzzles correspond to sl₃ weights while standard growth diagrams use type A structures points toward a resolution.

**Synthesis proposal**: KTW puzzles require **quantum sl₃ Yang-Baxter equations at roots of unity**, not the classical type A equations typically assumed. The triangular geometry isn't an obstacle—it's the natural setting for sl₃ representation theory.

This reframes the problem: instead of forcing puzzles into type A vertex models, we should develop **type A₂ growth diagrams** where triangular geometry is native, not adapted.

## Information Theory as the Decisive Test

The computational analysis provides the clearest refutation criterion: **4:1 compression with constant-size labels is information-theoretically impossible** for maintaining bijective structure. This mathematical impossibility explains why all previous attempts have failed.

However, this creates an interesting tension with the vertex model specialist's suggestion of "interacting integrable models." Could such models provide the **variable-size face labels** needed to handle exponential compression ratios? This would require abandoning the classical growth diagram framework entirely.

## Alternative Framework: Constraint Propagation Networks

The algebraic specialist's counter-proposal—constraint propagation networks—deserves serious consideration. Instead of spatial locality, these would encode **dependency locality** where information flows along constraint relationships rather than geometric adjacency.

**Synthetic insight**: Puzzles and tableaux might both be **projection shadows** of a higher-dimensional constraint network. The migration algorithm would then represent **dimensional reduction** rather than temporal evolution. Growth diagrams might exist in this higher-dimensional space, with puzzles and tableaux as lower-dimensional slices.

## The Categorical Structure Gap

The algebraic specialist identifies that growth diagrams require **monoidal categories** where objects can be tensored, but KTW puzzles don't compose this way due to rigid triangular geometry. This reveals why attempts to extend growth diagrams to puzzles feel forced.

**Resolution pathway**: Instead of modifying growth diagrams for puzzles, develop **triangular lattice combinatorics** as an independent mathematical structure. The puzzle-tableau bijection might require **category bridging** rather than internal equivalence.
